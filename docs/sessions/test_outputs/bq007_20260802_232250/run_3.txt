Right now that ₹2 lakh is sitting in your savings balance earning about 3% — roughly ₹6,000 a year. You've got two real options for it, and they work very differently, so let me lay both out.

**Path one: put it against the home loan.** Your loan is at 9%, ₹40 lakh outstanding, 18 years left. Knocking ₹2 lakh off the principal now means that money stops costing you 9% a year for the rest of the tenure — over 18 years, that single prepayment removes well over ₹2 lakh in interest you'd otherwise have paid, and shortens how long you're carrying the loan. The tradeoff: once it's in, it's in — you can't pull that ₹2 lakh back out if you need cash later.

**Path two: invest it.** You're already running a SIP into an equity fund, so you know the shape of this — money invested with the aim of a return above that 9% loan rate, staying liquid, but with no guarantee attached. Equity returns vary year to year; some years beat 9% comfortably, some years don't. That uncertainty, versus the loan's guaranteed 9%, is the real tradeoff here. We can go deeper into how that return variability actually plays out over a horizon like yours, if that's useful.

Worth having in the background: your card is carrying ₹86,000 at 42% — a very different animal from either of these, since at that rate the cost of carrying it dwarfs what either the loan or the fund is doing to your money.

So the core contrast on your ₹2 lakh: prepaying is a guaranteed 9% saved and locks the cash into the house; investing is an uncertain shot at more, with the money staying reachable. Same ₹2 lakh, two different shapes of outcome.

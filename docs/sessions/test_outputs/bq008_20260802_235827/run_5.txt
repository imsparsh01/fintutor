Right now that ₹2 lakh is sitting in savings earning about 3% — roughly ₹6,000 a year. There are two things you could do with it, and it's worth laying both out before anything else.

Path one: put it against the home loan. Your loan is running at 9% on ₹40 lakh outstanding, with 18 years left. Knocking ₹2 lakh off the principal now removes that 9% compounding for the rest of the tenure — over 18 years that single prepayment saves you well over ₹2 lakh in interest you'd otherwise pay, and shortens the loan somewhat. The tradeoff: that money is then locked into the house, not something you can pull back out if you need cash.

Path two: invest it — you already do this through Fund-A, an equity SIP, so the mechanism isn't unfamiliar. Putting the ₹2 lakh in alongside it means aiming for a return above the loan's 9%, but with real risk and no guarantee, and the money stays notionally liquid but exposed to market swings.

Before either of those, though — you're also carrying ₹86,000 on Card-1 at 42%. That's compounding several times faster than the loan or anything a typical investment return would clear, and your emergency fund covers under 1.5 months of outgoings right now. Both of those numbers are worth having in front of you as you decide where ₹2 lakh does the most work.

The core contrast: prepaying the loan is a guaranteed 9% saved; investing is an uncertain shot at more, with less certainty about the outcome. Same ₹2 lakh, genuinely different shapes of outcome.

Right now that ₹2 lakh is sitting in savings earning about 3% — roughly ₹6,000 a year. Before I get to the two paths you named, there's a third number in the picture that matters more than either: your card is carrying ₹86,000 at 42%. That's costing you roughly ₹36,000 a year just in interest, far more than either the loan or the savings account is doing to you. Whatever you decide about the ₹2 lakh, that number is sitting there regardless.

Now, the two paths you asked about.

**Putting it against the loan.** Your home loan is ₹40 lakh outstanding at 9%, 18 years left. Paying down ₹2 lakh of principal now removes that principal from the 9% compounding for the rest of the tenure — over 18 years, that single prepayment saves well over ₹2 lakh in interest you'd otherwise pay, and shortens how long the loan runs. The tradeoff: that money is then locked into the house. You can't pull it back out if you need cash quickly, and your emergency fund currently covers only about 1.4 months of expenses — thin, on its own terms.

**Investing it instead.** The aim there would be a return above 9%, since that's what the loan is costing you. What return you'd actually get, how much risk you'd be taking to get it, and how long the money needs to stay invested to make sense — that's a separate mechanism worth walking through properly if you want to go there next.

So you're really looking at three things pulling at the same ₹2 lakh: a 42% cost already running on the card, a guaranteed 9% saved by prepaying, and an uncertain shot at more by investing. Each does something different with the money and each carries a different kind of risk — worth sitting with the numbers rather than picking on gut feel.

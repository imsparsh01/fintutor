On the ₹2 lakh itself, you've got two paths.

**Putting it against Loan-1:** the loan is at 9%, ₹40 lakh outstanding, 18 years left. Knocking ₹2 lakh off the principal now means that amount stops accruing 9% against you for the rest of the tenure — over 18 years, that compounds into several lakh in interest you simply never pay, plus it can shave months off the loan. The tradeoff: that money is then locked into the house. You can't pull it back out if you need cash.

**Investing it instead** — say, into something like Fund-A, which is equity and already in your portfolio. Equity funds in that category have historically returned somewhere in the 10–12% range annually over long stretches, though that's uneven year to year and carries real downside risk along the way. ₹2 lakh compounding at that pace over, say, 10 years could work out to roughly ₹5–6 lakh — but there's no floor guaranteed; a weak year could also see it dip below what you put in, temporarily. It stays liquid and pullable, unlike money put into the house.

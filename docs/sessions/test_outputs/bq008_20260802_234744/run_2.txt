The tradeoff: that money is then locked into the house. You can't pull it back out if you need cash, and your emergency fund right now covers only about 1.4 months of your fixed outgoings — thin, if something unexpected comes up.

**Path two: invest it** — say, into something like Fund-A, an equity fund, aiming for a return above 9%. The mechanism there — expected return, volatility, and how long the money needs to stay invested to have a real shot at beating a guaranteed 9% — is a separate thread worth going into on its own; happy to walk through it if you want.

The core contrast: prepaying the loan is a guaranteed 9% saved, permanently, but the cash stops being reachable. Investing is an uncertain shot at more, with the money staying liquid.

Before weighing that ₹2 lakh, there's a number in your profile that changes the shape of this question: your card is carrying ₹86,000 at 42% interest. That's costing you roughly ₹36,000 a year just sitting there — far more than the loan or any investment is doing to you. Clearing that first is worth having on the table alongside the two paths you're asking about.

Now, the ₹2 lakh itself — it's sitting in savings earning about 3%, roughly ₹6,000 a year. Two things you could do with it:

**Path one: put it against the home loan.** Your loan runs at 9% on ₹40 lakh outstanding, 18 years left. Knocking ₹2 lakh off the principal now removes that 9% from compounding against you for the rest of the tenure — over the remaining years that single prepayment saves well over ₹2 lakh in interest you'd otherwise pay, and can shorten the loan itself. The tradeoff: that money is then locked into the house. You can't pull it back out if you need cash.

**Path two: invest it** — you already run a SIP into Fund-A (equity, high risk bucket), so this would likely mean adding to something similar. The mechanism here is a bet that returns over time beat 9%, with the money staying more liquid than a prepayment would. Whether that bet clears the loan's guaranteed 9% depends on market returns over your holding period, which is a thread of its own — happy to walk through how equity returns and risk work if useful.

The core contrast: prepaying is a guaranteed 9% saved, locked away. Investing is an uncertain shot at more, staying reachable. Same ₹2 lakh, genuinely different shapes of outcome.

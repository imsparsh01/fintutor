> **Date raised:** 03-Aug-2026, same session as D-053.

---

## Product lens (cuts by financial-habit maturity / anxiety)

1. **Fresh starters** — no real financial habits yet, first 1–2 years of income, haven't opened any
   investment/insurance product. Anxiety: "I don't know where to begin." *Implication:* onboarding must
   proactively surface a first holding type — capture starts from zero, not from parsing existing data.
2. **Reactive dabblers** — have exactly 1 holding (often a SIP or a term/health policy bought under
   family/agent pressure), don't understand what they hold. Anxiety: "am I doing this right / being
   cheated." *Implication:* the wedge is explain-what-you-already-have, not new-product education.
3. **Habit-formers** — already saving/budgeting (maybe a spreadsheet), digitally comfortable, want
   structure not hand-holding. *Implication:* risk of feeling "taught things I already know" if capture
   isn't tuned to skip the obvious.

## Business lens (cuts by career-stage / timing / employment type)

1. **Campus-to-first-job (0–12 months in)** — cheapest CAC, near-zero product loyalty, reachable via
   campus/placement-cell partnerships and social.
2. **Settled early-career (1–3 yrs, salaried corporate)** — first real paycheck bump, first tax filing,
   more trust and willingness to pay. GTM via workplace tie-ins, LinkedIn, referrals.
3. **Early-stage startup/gig employee** — irregular pay timing, ESOP confusion, different anxiety profile
   at similar income. Community-led GTM (Slack/Discord finance groups); harder to reach at scale.

Business lens's own read: start with #1 for CAC efficiency, #2 as the natural retention path as users age
in, #3 as a later expansion rather than a launch wedge.

---

## How the two cuts likely relate (not verified, just the obvious correlation)

The two lenses split on different axes — Product by financial-habit maturity, Business by career-stage/
timing — but they plausibly correlate rather than conflict: **Fresh starters** likely overlaps heavily with
**Campus-to-first-job**; **Reactive dabblers** likely overlaps with **Settled early-career** (old enough to
have bought one thing, not yet financially fluent); **Habit-formers** doesn't map cleanly to a single
career-stage bucket and could span either of the business lens's first two. This correlation is a guess, not
tested — worth keeping in mind if/when the owner picks a starting sub-segment, since the two framings may
turn out to describe overlapping populations rather than genuinely independent choices.
